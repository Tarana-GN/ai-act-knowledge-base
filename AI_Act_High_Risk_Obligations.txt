================================================================
DOCUMENT 3: AI ACT - HIGH-RISK AI SYSTEM OBLIGATIONS
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689
Applicable from: August 2026
================================================================

Providers of high-risk AI systems must comply with all obligations below
BEFORE placing the system on the market. Deployers must comply with
operational obligations during use.

================================================================
ARTICLE 9 - RISK MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
Providers must establish, implement, document, and maintain a risk
management system throughout the entire lifecycle of the high-risk AI system.

KEY ELEMENTS:
- Continuous iterative process: identification, analysis, estimation,
  evaluation, and adoption of risk management measures
- Identification of known and reasonably foreseeable risks
- Estimation of risks that may emerge from reasonably foreseeable misuse
- Evaluation of risks based on post-market monitoring data
- Testing to identify appropriate risk management measures

RISK MANAGEMENT MEASURES must:
- Be proportionate to the risk level
- Consider the state of the art
- Take into account effects on children and vulnerable groups
- Address residual risks communicated to deployers via instructions

TESTING:
- Must be performed at appropriate times during development
- Must be against pre-defined metrics and probabilistic thresholds
- Testing on real-world conditions where appropriate

================================================================
ARTICLE 10 - DATA AND DATA GOVERNANCE
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
High-risk AI systems that use training, validation, and testing data must
apply appropriate data governance and management practices.

KEY ELEMENTS:
Training, validation, and testing datasets must:
- Be subject to data governance and management practices
- Be relevant, representative, and free from errors to the best extent possible
- Have appropriate statistical properties regarding persons or groups of persons
- Take into account characteristics of the specific geographic, contextual,
  behavioural, or functional setting where the system is intended to be used

DATA GOVERNANCE MUST COVER:
- Relevant design choices
- Data collection processes and origin of data
- Relevant data preparation operations (annotation, labelling, cleaning,
  enrichment, aggregation, de-identification)
- Formulation of assumptions regarding the data
- Assessment of availability, quantity, and suitability of data
- Examination for possible biases
- Identification of relevant data gaps or shortcomings

SPECIAL CATEGORY DATA:
Processing of special categories of personal data (race, health, etc.) for
bias monitoring and correction is allowed under strict conditions.

================================================================
ARTICLE 11 - TECHNICAL DOCUMENTATION
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
Before placing on the market, providers must draw up technical documentation
demonstrating compliance with all requirements.

CONTENT MUST INCLUDE:
- General description of the AI system and its intended purpose
- Description of the system's components and development process
- Description of the architecture, design choices, and key design parameters
- Training methodologies, techniques, and algorithms used
- Description of the training data and data governance practices
- Assessment of measures for human oversight
- Description of validation and testing procedures and outcomes
- Known and foreseeable risks of the high-risk AI system
- Description of any changes made during the lifecycle
- List of harmonised standards applied
- Copy of EU declaration of conformity

Documentation must be kept for 10 years after the last system placed on market.
Format: Annex IV of the AI Act provides the full template.

================================================================
ARTICLE 12 - RECORD-KEEPING (LOGGING)
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
High-risk AI systems must automatically record events (logs) throughout
their operational lifetime to enable post-market monitoring.

LOGGING MUST ENABLE:
- Traceability of the AI system's functioning throughout its lifetime
- Monitoring for compliance with the regulation
- Appropriate level of traceability for the intended purpose
- Detection of situations leading to risks or substantial modifications

MINIMUM LOG CONTENT:
- Recording of the period of each use (start and end date/time)
- Reference database against which input data was checked
- Input data that led to the system's output
- Identity of the natural persons involved in the result verification

Storage period: minimum 6 months unless other EU/national law specifies longer.

================================================================
ARTICLE 13 - TRANSPARENCY AND PROVISION OF INFORMATION TO DEPLOYERS
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
High-risk AI systems must be designed to operate transparently and with
sufficient instructions of use for deployers.

INFORMATION PROVIDED TO DEPLOYERS MUST INCLUDE:
- Identity and contact details of the provider
- Characteristics, capabilities, and limitations of the system
  (intended purpose, level of accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity)
- Known or foreseeable circumstances under which the system may fail or
  produce incorrect outputs
- Performance metrics
- Hardware and software requirements
- Description of any form of human oversight
- Expected lifetime and maintenance/care measures
- Description of data preprocessing requirements

The instructions must be in a language easily understood by deployers.
They must be made available before the system is put into service.

================================================================
ARTICLE 14 - HUMAN OVERSIGHT
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
High-risk AI systems must be designed and developed to allow effective
oversight by natural persons during the period of use.

HUMAN OVERSIGHT MUST ENABLE PERSONS TO:
a) Fully understand the capabilities and limitations of the AI system
b) Monitor operation of the AI system and detect anomalies, dysfunctions,
   and unexpected performance
c) Remain aware of possible automation bias (over-relying on AI output)
d) Correctly interpret the AI system's output
e) Decide NOT to use the AI system or override, ignore or reverse its output
f) Intervene in the operation and STOP the system via a "stop" button or
   similar procedure

SPECIFIC OVERSIGHT MEASURES:
- Must be appropriate to the risks, level of autonomy, and context
- Must be identifiable and implemented BEFORE placing on the market
- Deployers must assign qualified personnel to perform oversight
- Must include ability to interrupt the system if needed

================================================================
ARTICLE 15 - ACCURACY, ROBUSTNESS, AND CYBERSECURITY
================================================================

REQUIREMENT:
High-risk AI systems must achieve an appropriate level of accuracy,
robustness, and cybersecurity throughout their lifecycle.

ACCURACY:
- Declared level of accuracy must be stated in technical documentation
- Accuracy metrics relevant to the intended purpose must be used
- Must be measured using appropriate test datasets

ROBUSTNESS:
Systems must be resilient against:
- Errors, faults, or inconsistencies in the AI system or its environment
- Attempts by third parties to alter use, outputs, or performance (adversarial
  robustness)
- Data or model biases
- Technical limitations (e.g., insufficient data)

Fail-safe fallback plans must be implementable.

CYBERSECURITY:
- Must be resilient against attempts to exploit vulnerabilities
- Protection against data poisoning attacks
- Protection against model poisoning attacks
- Protection against adversarial examples (inputs designed to deceive the AI)
- Technical solutions must be proportionate to the risk

================================================================
CONFORMITY ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES
================================================================

SELF-ASSESSMENT (Most high-risk AI - Annex III):
- Provider conducts internal conformity assessment
- Must verify compliance with all Articles 9-15
- Must draw up EU declaration of conformity (Article 47)
- Must affix CE marking (Article 48)
- Must register in EU database (Article 49)

THIRD-PARTY ASSESSMENT (Certain high-risk AI):
Required for:
- Biometric identification systems (unless post-remote, real-time prohibited)
- Critical infrastructure AI
- AI in medical devices and safety components of products (Annex I)
Must involve a notified body (accredited third-party conformity assessment body)

EU DECLARATION OF CONFORMITY:
- Signed by provider before placing on market
- States that all applicable requirements are met
- Must be kept for 10 years
- Must be made available to national authorities

CE MARKING:
- Affixed visibly on the AI system or its documentation
- Indicates conformity with all applicable EU requirements
- Required before placing on market or putting into service

================================================================
DEPLOYER OBLIGATIONS (Summary)
================================================================

Deployers of high-risk AI systems must:
- Use the system in accordance with instructions of use
- Assign human oversight to qualified and trained persons
- Monitor operation and report serious incidents to the provider
- Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessment if required under GDPR
- Inform employees and their representatives about use of AI in the workplace
- Keep logs generated by the AI system for at least 6 months
- Not use the system for purposes other than those intended
